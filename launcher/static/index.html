RapidCache Docs

              

                What RapidCache does, and what your download speed is telling you.
              

            

          


          

            
What RapidCache is

            

              Your pod has one internet connection. Think of it as a pipe.
            

            

              Model files come from Hugging Face, and Hugging Face allows
              one tap on that pipe per file. Their rule. There is no way
              around it.
            

            

              RapidCache holds the same files, byte for byte, on storage that allows
              sixteen.
            

            

              Why that matters when things are busy. Your pod shares its
              server with other people's pods. One of them starts a big download and there
              is less left for everyone, you included. It finishes and yours comes back.
            

            

              Sixteen weak taps still fill a bucket sixteen times faster than one.
            


            
What a pod actually is

            

              You rent a GPU. The internet connection and the storage belong to the
              machine it sits in, shared with every other pod on that machine. You cannot
              see them. Neither can we.
            

            

              There is no such thing as a bad pod, only a busy server. The same pod can be
              both inside ten minutes.
            


            
Why is my download only 80 MB/s?

            

              
              
            

            
That number is your pod, not the service.

            

              Two pods, same 11.46 GB file, same moment. Both busy. One signed in to
              RapidCache, one not.
            

            

              

                
                  
tapsspeedthat one file

                
                
                  

                    RapidCache16
                    97.2 MB/sabout 2 minutes
                  

                  

                    Standard1
                    6.2 MB/sabout 31 minutes
                  

                
              

            

            

              97 MB/s is what RapidCache managed on a machine that could spare 6 MB/s for
              a single connection.
            

            

              Nothing is broken when this happens. Every connection stays
              open and working. You get no error because there is no error, just smaller
              numbers, and they climb back when the busy pod finishes.
            


            
What good and bad look like

            

              
              
              
              
            

            
All four are real installs. Our own measurements:

              We have watched one file move 306 to 49 to 233 MB/s in a few
              minutes, nothing changing at either end. One reading tells you
              nothing.
            


            
When to wait, and when to start a new pod

            
    
              
  • 
                A slow reading in the first minute or two: ignore it.
                Connections need a few seconds to reach full speed and a 300 MB file is
                over before they get there. Small files always read lower.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Still under 150 MB/s after several minutes, with no sign of
                recovery: you are on a busy machine. Stop the pod and deploy a new
                one. You will land on a different machine.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Do not restart immediately. Redeploying usually costs more
                time than waiting would have, and waiting is free. We have seen pods recover
                fivefold on their own.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Not sure? Press Debug. It tells you what
                your pod actually did.
              
    • 
            


            
Cancelling, and picking up where you left off

            

              Cancel stops the install. It does not throw away what has already come down.
            

            

              It takes effect immediately, including during custom node installs, where
              things otherwise sit for minutes.
            

            

              Press the same workflow again on the same pod and it carries on. Finished
              files are checked rather than fetched again, and a half-finished one picks up
              where it stopped. Nothing comes down twice.
            

            
Two things worth knowing:

            
    
              
  • 
                This holds on the same pod. Deploy a new one and the
                part-finished file is gone with the old pod's temporary storage. Models that
                already finished survive only if /workspace is a Network
                volume.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                A part-finished file is kept on the pod and cleared automatically after a
                day.
              
    • 
            

            

              So if the server is busy, cancelling costs you nothing. Wait a few minutes and
              press the tile again.
            


            
What the numbers mean

            

              
              
            

            
An install does two different things, and both show a speed:

            
    
              
  • 
                Downloading: pulling the file from the internet. This is
                where your pod's connection matters.
              
    • 
              
  • 
                Saving to disk: copying the finished file onto your pod's
                storage. No internet involved, so it usually reads much higher.
              
    • 
            

            

              Seeing 77 MB/s and then 1.10 GB/s a moment later is normal. Different job,
              different number.
            


            
Setting up your pod

            
            

              Container disk. Temporary space, wiped when the pod stops.
              Downloads are staged here first. The largest single model is
              28.43 GB, so give it 100 GB or more. Our
              template ships 200 GB.
            

            
Storage for your models. RunPod offers two:

            

              

                
                  

                    Volume disk
                    local to your pod
                    Fastest. Deleted when the pod is terminated.
                  

                  

                    Network volume
                    shared storage
                    
                      Survives termination, mountable by several pods, cheaper. About ten
                      times slower to write.
                    
                  

                
              

            

            

              Size. Dataset Generator is 44.6 GB and MiniMax H3 is 63.4 GB,
              plus ComfyUI itself. 200 GB is comfortable for two or three
              workflows. 100 GB is tight.
            

            

              Environment variables. RunPod adds
              PUBLIC_KEY and JUPYTER_PASSWORD to every pod
              automatically, for its own SSH and Jupyter. RapidCache needs none. Sign in on
              the RapidCache tab instead.
            


            
Signing in

            

              
              
            

            

              Open the RapidCache tab, enter the email and password from

What success looks like

            
            

              100%, and Workflow ready. If it says a number of skipped
              items, open the warning. The install finished, and one optional step did not.
              It is usually safe to continue.
            


            
The Debug button

            
            

              If something looks wrong, press Debug. It writes a report of
              what your pod actually did: how fast each file downloaded, how long each step
              took, what kind of storage you are on, and anything that failed.
            

            

              No passwords, no links, no file locations. Safe to paste anywhere, and it
              beats describing the problem.
            


            
What we promise

            
RapidCache cannot make your server less busy. Nobody can.

            

              What it does is take everything your pod has to give, instead of the
              fraction a single connection would get.
            

            

              That is the promise. Not a number, because nobody can honestly promise a
              number on shared hardware.
